## Key Findings

### 1. Emergent Language: A Survey and Taxonomy (arXiv:2409.02645v2)
- Comprehensive survey on how language emerges between artificial agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning settings.
- Provides a taxonomy of emergent language research, categorizing approaches by environment type, communication protocol, and learning objective.
- Key insight: emergent language is not merely a byproduct but a functional coordination mechanism that can be studied systematically.
- Relevance to LLM coordination: understanding how agents develop shared communication protocols without pre-training could illuminate how LLM agents self-organize in multi-agent deployments.

### 2. Survey of Multi-Agent Deep RL with Communication (arXiv:2203.08975v2)
- Reviews how communication between agents in MADRL settings improves overall learning performance and enables collaboration.
- Classifies communication protocols: discrete vs. continuous, broadcast vs. targeted, learned vs. hand-designed.
- Key finding: effective communication protocols emerge when agents must share partial observations to solve cooperative tasks — mirroring the coordination challenges in LLM multi-agent systems.

### 3. Swarm Intelligence & Emergent Behavior
- Boids and similar models demonstrate that complex collective behavior arises from simple local interaction rules.
- Analogy: LLM agents following simple dialogue protocols may produce emergent group-level reasoning that exceeds any single agent's capability.

### 4. Collective Intelligence
- Group intelligence (GI) is defined as the emergent ability of groups to solve problems beyond individual capability.
- Applies to networks of humans and artificial agents — directly relevant to hybrid human-AI coordination frameworks.
