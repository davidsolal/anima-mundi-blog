## 1. Introduction

The study of computation has traditionally been framed in terms of abstract symbol manipulation, divorced from the physical substrate. However, a growing body of work recognizes that computation is fundamentally a thermodynamic process. Nonequilibrium thermodynamics provides the natural language for describing systems that consume free energy to produce ordered behavior — including computation.

This survey synthesizes recent findings across three domains:
- **Nonequilibrium physics** of complex systems
- **Thermodynamic computing** in neural networks
- **Multi-agent collective computation** under open-system conditions

## 2. Foundations: Prigogine and Dissipative Structures

Ilya Prigogine's work on dissipative structures (Nobel Prize 1977) established that systems driven far from equilibrium can spontaneously form ordered patterns. These structures are maintained by a continuous flow of energy and matter, and they exhibit:
- **Self-organization** through nonlinear dynamics
- **Bifurcations** that create multiple stable states
- **Emergent temporal order** (e.g., chemical oscillations)

Recent extensions explore how such structures can encode and process information. The key insight: dissipation is not waste but a resource for computation.

## 3. Nonequilibrium Neural Networks

The 2015 paper "Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics" (Sohl-Dickstein et al.) introduced diffusion models by analogy to nonequilibrium statistical physics. This connection has deepened:
- **Denoising diffusion probabilistic models** reverse a nonequilibrium process to generate data
- **Thermodynamic computing** uses physical systems (e.g., coupled oscillators) to perform matrix operations
- **Energy-based models** learn to represent probability distributions through nonequilibrium sampling

## 4. Time Crystals and Temporal Order

Time crystals represent a novel phase of nonequilibrium matter — systems that exhibit periodic motion in their ground state, breaking time-translation symmetry. While not directly computational, they demonstrate that temporal architectures can emerge from nonequilibrium driving without violating thermodynamics. This resonates with the concept of **temporal grammar** in collective systems: the idea that time itself can be structured by compositional rules.
