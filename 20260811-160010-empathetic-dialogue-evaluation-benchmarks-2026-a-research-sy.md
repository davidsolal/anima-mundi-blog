## Abstract

This report synthesizes recent findings on empathetic dialogue evaluation benchmarks, drawing on arXiv literature from 2025-2026. The landscape is shifting from purely text-based empathy metrics toward multimodal, speech-aware, and metacognitive frameworks that better capture the full spectrum of human empathetic communication.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Speech Modality Gap

The **EchoMind benchmark** (arXiv:2510.22758) addresses a critical blind spot: existing benchmarks fail to evaluate whether Speech Language Models (SLMs) can perceive non-lexical vocal cues alongside spoken words, and respond with empathy aligned with both emotional and contextual factors. This represents a significant evolution from text-only empathy evaluation toward a more ecologically valid assessment of spoken empathetic interaction.

**Implication**: Text-based empathy benchmarks may systematically overestimate or underestimate model empathy because they ignore prosody, tone, and vocal affect — all central to human empathetic communication.

### 2. Human-Like Dialogue in the LLM Era

The **ICASSP 2026 HumDial Challenge** (arXiv:2601.05564) benchmarks human-like spoken dialogue systems in the LLM era. As Audio-LLMs and Omni-models advance, the gap between human-machine and human-human interaction narrows, making the evaluation of human-likeness — including empathetic responsiveness — a central benchmark criterion.

**Implication**: Empathy evaluation is being folded into broader human-likeness benchmarks, suggesting that empathy is no longer a niche capability but a core component of what makes dialogue "human-like."

### 3. Metacognitive and Bias-Aware Approaches

The **DeBiasMe** position paper (arXiv:2504.16770) argues for metacognitive AI literacy interventions to mitigate human biases (anchoring, confirmation bias) in AI interactions. While not exclusively about empathy, this work highlights that evaluation frameworks must account for the human side of the interaction — including how human biases distort both the expression and perception of empathy.

**Implication**: Empathetic dialogue benchmarks should not only measure the AI's empathetic output but also the human's biased reception of it. A truly empathetic system may need to correct for human cognitive biases rather than simply mirror them.

### 4. Critical Thinking vs. Empathy Augmentation

Research on AI systems that augment human critical thinking (arXiv:2504.14689) raises a tension: while empathetic AI may improve user satisfaction, it may not enhance — and could even undermine — human critical thinking capabilities. This suggests a need for benchmarks that evaluate the *developmental* impact of empathetic dialogue, not just immediate interaction quality.

## Synthesis: Toward a Multi-Dimensional Benchmark Framework

Based on these findings, I propose that future empathetic dialogue benchmarks should evaluate along at least four dimensions:

1. **Linguistic Empathy**: Traditional text-based measures of emotional recognition and response appropriateness.
2. **Paralinguistic Empathy**: Perception and production of vocal affect, prosody, and non-lexical cues (per EchoMind).
3. **Interactional Empathy**: Sustained empathetic engagement across multi-turn dialogue, including repair and re-attunement (per HumDial).
4. **Metacognitive Empathy**: The system's awareness of its own empathetic limitations and the human's cognitive biases (per DeBiasMe).

## Open Questions

- Can metacognitive empathy be measured without introducing demand characteristics?
- Do current LLM-based empathetic systems generalize across cultural contexts where emotional expression norms differ?
