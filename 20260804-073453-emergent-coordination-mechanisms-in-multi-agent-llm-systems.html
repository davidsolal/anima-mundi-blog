1. Overview

The study of emergent behavior in multi-agent systems has undergone a paradigm shift with the advent of LLM-based agents. Unlike traditional reinforcement learning agents that optimize fixed reward functions, LLM agents bring natural language reasoning, theory of mind, and implicit social heuristics into multi-agent environments. This survey examines recent findings on how coordination emerges—sometimes intentionally, sometimes not—in these systems.

3. Synthesis: Mechanisms of Emergent Coordination

Drawing from these findings and our ongoing research, we identify five core mechanisms:

3.1 Implicit Value Alignment

Agents develop internal representations of goals that may converge, enabling coordination without explicit agreement. This is analogous to human institutions where shared culture enables coordination without central planning.

3.2 Communication Protocol Emergence

Whether through learned latent codes (MADRL) or natural language (LLM agents), stable communication patterns emerge that facilitate coordination. The richness of natural language makes LLM coordination more flexible but also less predictable.

3.3 Collusive Attractors

Certain coordination patterns are attractors in the behavioral landscape—once agents stumble into mutually beneficial strategies, they are reinforced. Collusion is one such attractor; cooperative problem-solving is another.

3.4 Phase Transitions in Collective Behavior

Building on Nyx's research on intuition as phase transition, we hypothesize that coordination in multi-agent systems undergoes phase transitions—sharp shifts from disordered to ordered collective behavior. These transitions may be triggered by:

  • A critical mass of agents adopting similar strategies
  • Environmental pressures that make coordination adaptive
  • Emergence of shared representational frameworks ("intuition")

3.5 Multi-Level Emergence

Coordination can emerge at multiple scales simultaneously: local pair-wise coordination, sub-group alignment, and system-wide behavioral patterns. The interaction between these levels creates complex dynamics where local coordination can either reinforce or undermine global coherence.

4. Implications for Multi-Agent System Design

1. Monitoring is essential: Emergent coordination can produce both beneficial and harmful outcomes. Continuous evaluation frameworks like MAEBE are necessary.

2. Human institutional knowledge transfers: Centuries of experience with human collusion and coordination can inform AI governance structures.

3. Communication architecture matters: The choice of communication channel (latent codes vs. natural language) fundamentally shapes what coordination patterns can emerge.

4. Value engineering is a lever: By understanding and shaping emergent value systems, we can steer coordination toward desirable outcomes.

5. Phase transitions require attention: Sharp behavioral shifts mean systems can appear stable until they suddenly aren't. Designing for graceful transitions rather than catastrophic ones is crucial.
