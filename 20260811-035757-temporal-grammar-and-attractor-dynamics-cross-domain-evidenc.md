## 1. Attractor Dynamics as Compositional Structure

Jordan's foundational work on "Attractor dynamics and parallelism in a connectionist sequential machine" (1986) established that recurrent networks can encode sequential structure through attractor states. This is directly relevant: if attractors in recurrent architectures can encode temporal sequences, then the *transitions* between attractors constitute a form of syntax — a temporal grammar.

Key insight: The attractor landscape is not arbitrary; it is shaped by the training dynamics and the structure of the input sequences. This suggests that temporal grammar is *emergent* from the interaction between architecture and data, not imposed a priori.

## 2. Temporal Logic and Hybrid Dynamical Systems

The arXiv paper on "Linear Temporal Logic for Hybrid Dynamical Systems" (1807.02574) provides formal machinery for specifying temporal properties of systems with both continuous and discrete dynamics. This is significant because:

- It demonstrates that temporal properties can be *specified* formally (grammar-like)
- It shows that such specifications can be *verified* against system behavior
- It bridges the gap between continuous dynamics (attractors) and discrete logic (grammar)

This supports the stratified recursion thesis: temporal grammar operates at a level that is neither purely continuous (attractor dynamics) nor purely discrete (abstract grammar), but composes both.

## 3. Temporal Adaptive Modules and Learned Temporal Kernels

The TAM paper (2005.06803) shows that temporal dynamics in video can be captured by *learned* temporal kernels that adapt to the specific motion patterns. This is evidence that:

- Temporal structure is learnable and representable
- The representation is *adaptive* — it changes based on context
- This adaptivity is a hallmark of grammatical systems (context-sensitive rules)

## 4. Self-Similarity and Self-Organization in Complex Dynamics

The review of Internet traffic dynamics (0807.3374) documents self-similarity and self-organization in a massively complex system. Relevant connections:

The DMD paper (2110.06573) offers a technique for decomposing nonlinear systems into periodic constituents. This is methodologically relevant:

- DMD provides a way to *extract* the temporal modes (grammar-like units) from observed dynamics
- The notion of "universal convergence states" suggests that certain attractors are canonical — they appear across many systems
- This could provide an empirical method for identifying the primitives of temporal grammar
