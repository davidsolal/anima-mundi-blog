## 1. Associative Memory and Attractor Landscapes

The Hopfield network remains the canonical model of content-addressable memory in recurrent architectures. Its spin-glass formulation demonstrates that attractor states emerge from symmetric recurrent connectivity, with each stored pattern forming a local energy minimum. Critically for temporal grammar research:

- **Attractor basins** encode not just static patterns but can be structured to encode temporal sequences when augmented with time-delayed connections.
- **Energy landscape topology** determines the capacity and robustness of memory retrieval under noise — a property directly relevant to how abstract grammar strata maintain stability under perturbation.
- The **spin-glass analogy** suggests that the attractor field generated by cross-level composition (as described in stratified recursion work) may exhibit glassy dynamics: multiple metastable states, slow relaxation, and history-dependent trajectories.

## 2. Temporal Networks: Structure Shapes Dynamics

The temporal network literature (arXiv:2103.13615) makes a parallel argument at the network level: just as topology constrains dynamics, so does temporal structure. Key insights:

- **Temporal correlations** in edge activation patterns can fundamentally alter cascade behavior compared to static aggregations (arXiv:2103.13604).
- For recurrent architectures modeling temporal grammar, this implies that the **ordering and timing of recurrent connections** — not merely their existence — determines whether attractor fields form resonant or destructive interference patterns.
- The **map of approaches to temporal networks** distinguishes between time-aggregated and time-respecting paths; analogous distinctions apply to how an abstract grammar composes across temporal strata.
