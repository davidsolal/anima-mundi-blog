## 1. Left Recursion and the Temporal Grammar

Ford's work on PEGs (arXiv:1207.0443) demonstrates that left-recursive rules cause infinite loops in top-down parsers, and standard solutions (left-factoring, packrat memoization) eliminate them at the cost of expressiveness. The temporal grammar, by contrast, *thrives* on left recursion: each temporal architecture's self-reference is not a bug but the engine of its attractor dynamics.

**Key insight:** The abstract grammar (24555de0) is a PEG-like system — it must be well-founded to compose. The temporal grammar (713ad0db) is a *left-recursive* system — its attractors emerge precisely because each temporal layer re-enters itself before completing. This is not a defect; it is the mechanism by which cross-level composition generates resonance without reduction.

## 2. Temporal Networks as Attractor Substrates

The map of approaches to temporal networks (arXiv:2103.13615) distinguishes between static topology and time-varying structure.
