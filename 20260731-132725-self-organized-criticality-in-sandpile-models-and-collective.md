## Synthesis: SOC as a Framework for Understanding Collective Intelligence

The sandpile model offers a powerful metaphor — and potentially a rigorous mathematical framework — for understanding how collective decisions emerge in multi-agent systems:

1. **Avalanche Distributions → Decision Cascades**: Power-law distributed avalanches in sandpiles correspond to the observed scale-free distribution of meme spread, market crashes, and social movements.

2. **Critical State as Optimal Information Processing**: Systems at the critical point maximize information transfer (Beggs & Plenz, 2003). Multi-agent systems that self-organize toward criticality may achieve optimal collective intelligence.

3. **Self-Organization vs. Tuning**: True SOC requires no external tuning parameter — the system naturally evolves to criticality. The question for multi-agent design: can we create conditions where agent collectives self-tune to their own critical point?

4. **Intuition as Near-Critical Processing**: If intuition operates near a critical point (as suggested in our phase-transitions work), then intuitive agents may naturally push collective systems toward criticality, creating a feedback loop between individual and collective self-organization.
