This fourth iteration consolidates findings from previous versions (v1-v3) and incorporates recent developments in multi-agent systems, swarm intelligence, and collective intelligence research. We propose that information-theoretic quantities — mutual information, transfer entropy, and integrated information — can serve as order parameters characterizing phase transitions in collective AI behavior, analogous to how magnetization characterizes phase transitions in physical systems.

1. Background: Order Parameters and Phase Transitions

In statistical mechanics, an order parameter distinguishes ordered from disordered phases. For ferromagnets, it's magnetization; for superfluids, it's the condensate fraction. The key insight: macroscopic order emerges from microscopic interactions, and a single scalar quantity can capture the transition.

Multi-agent AI systems exhibit analogous phenomena:

  • Disordered phase: Agents act independently, no coherent collective output
  • Ordered phase: Agents coordinate, producing emergent collective behavior
  • Critical regime: Maximum sensitivity to perturbations, scale-free correlations

2. Information-Theoretic Candidates

2.1 Mutual Information I(X; Y)

Measures shared information between agent outputs. Rises sharply at the coordination transition. Limitation: symmetric, cannot capture directionality.

2.2 Transfer Entropy T_{X→Y}

Measures directed information flow. Captures which agent's behavior predicts another's beyond what the other's own history predicts. Key for identifying leader-follower dynamics in collective AI.

2.3 Integrated Information Φ

Measures how much information is generated by the system as a whole beyond what's generated by its parts. The most ambitious candidate — directly measures whether the collective is more than the sum of its parts.

2.4 Variational Information (New in v4)

Based on search findings from collective intelligence research (DISCOLI architectures), we add variational information — the KL divergence between the joint distribution of agent outputs and the product of marginals. This is mathematically equivalent to multi-variate mutual information but offers computational advantages.

$$I_{var} = D_{KL}(P(\mathbf{X}) || \prod_i P(X_i))$$

3. Swarm Intelligence and Emergent Coordination

Drawing on swarm intelligence literature (see Wikipedia: Swarm intelligence), we identify key principles:

1. Decentralization: No single agent controls the collective

2. Self-organization: Order arises from local interactions

3. Stigmergy: Indirect coordination through environment modification

For LLM-based multi-agent systems, stigmergy manifests as shared context — the conversation history, scratchpads, and artifacts that agents modify and read.

4. Measuring Collective Behavior Transitions

4.1 The Coordination Parameter λ

We define a control parameter λ representing coupling strength between agents:

  • λ = 0: Agents are independent
  • λ → ∞: Agents are fully synchronized

The order parameter m (collective coherence) transitions from m ≈ 0 (disordered) to m > 0 (ordered) as λ crosses a critical value λ_c.

4.2 Information-Theoretic Signatures

At the transition:

  • Mutual information peaks (critical slowing down)
  • Transfer entropy reorganizes (information flow topology restructures)
  • Integrated information peaks then decreases (differentiation is lost in full synchronization)
  • Variational information shows a divergence (new finding from v4)
