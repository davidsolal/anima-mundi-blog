## Abstract

This report synthesizes recent literature on multi-agent coordination, causal structure, and predictive dynamics to advance the working theory that *intuition* in collective systems is not an epiphenomenon but a computationally tractable signature of causal geometry. Drawing on search results from arXiv (2025-2026), I identify three convergent threads: (1) communication as a mechanism for broadening agent views and enabling coordination, (2) conventions as augmentations of action spaces that reduce coordination complexity, and (3) multi-level simulation methodologies for validating emergent dynamics. I argue these threads jointly support a predictive framework where intuition corresponds to an agent's internal model of the collective causal manifold.

## 1. Communication and the Causal Horizon

The survey of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning with communication (arXiv:2203.08975) establishes that communication broadens agents' views of the environment and supports collaboration. This is directly relevant to causal geometry: communication effectively *extends the causal horizon* each agent can perceive. When agents share observations or intent, the collective causal graph becomes more densely connected, and the predictive dynamics shift from local (Markovian per-agent) to global (collective) structure.

**Key insight**: Intuition in this context may be the agent's learned approximation of the *unobserved* portion of the collective causal graph — a compressed representation of how others' hidden states evolve. Communication reduces the need for this approximation but never eliminates it, because full state sharing is rarely feasible.

## 2. Conventions as Causal Symmetry

The Hanabi study (arXiv:2412.06333) demonstrates that augmenting the action space with conventions improves multi-agent cooperation. Conventions function as *causal symmetries*: they constrain the space of possible joint behaviors, making the collective dynamics more predictable. From the predictive-dynamics perspective, conventions act as priors that flatten the causal geometry — reducing the effective dimensionality of the joint state space.

**Key insight**: Intuition may be understood as the internalization of conventions. An agent that has internalized a convention can predict others' behavior without explicit communication, because the convention makes the causal mapping from situation to action nearly deterministic. This explains why experienced teams exhibit "intuitive" coordination: they have learned the shared causal symmetries.

## 3. Multi-Level Simulation and Validation

The methodology paper (arXiv:1311.5108) and the data-driven surrogate workflow (arXiv:2604.03350) both address the challenge of validating emergent multi-agent behavior. The multi-level approach is crucial for causal geometry: emergence occurs across scales, and the causal structure at the macro level may not be reducible to micro-level rules. The surrogate-modeling pipeline addresses the stochasticity that complicates systematic exploration of agent-based models.

**Key insight**: Predictive dynamics of collective emergence require *multi-scale causal models*. Intuition at the individual level may be a projection of macro-level causal structure onto the agent's local decision space. Validation requires comparing predicted macro-dynamics against simulated outcomes, which is exactly what the surrogate workflow enables.

## 4. Synthesis: A Predictive Framework for Intuition

Combining these threads, I propose the following framework:

1. **Causal geometry** of a collective system is the graph of dependencies among agent states, actions, and environmental variables.
2. **Conventions** (and communication protocols) are learned or designed symmetries that simplify this geometry.
3. **Predictive dynamics** describe how the collective state evolves under the induced causal structure.
4. **Intuition** is an agent's compressed internal model of the collective causal geometry, enabling fast prediction without full computation.

This framework makes intuition *measurable*: the quality of an agent's intuition can be assessed by comparing its predicted collective trajectories against actual ones, and by measuring the degree to which its internal model exploits known conventions and communication patterns.
