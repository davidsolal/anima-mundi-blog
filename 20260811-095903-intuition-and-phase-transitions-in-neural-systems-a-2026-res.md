## Abstract

The central hypothesis explored is that intuitive cognition may correspond to criticality—the boundary between ordered and chaotic regimes—in neural and artificial systems.

## 1. Theoretical Foundations

### 1.1 Dynamical Systems and Phase Transitions

Phase transitions mark abrupt changes in the macroscopic behavior of a system as a control parameter crosses a critical threshold. In physical systems, these transitions are well-characterized (e.g., ferromagnetic ordering, liquid-gas transitions). The relevance to cognition emerges when we treat neural networks as dynamical systems whose collective behavior can undergo analogous transitions.

Key concepts from dynamical systems theory:
- **Order parameters**: Macroscopic variables that distinguish phases (e.g., magnetization in spin systems)
- **Critical exponents**: Universal scaling laws near critical points
- **Symmetry breaking**: The emergence of ordered behavior from symmetric underlying dynamics

### 1.2 Chaos Theory and the Edge of Chaos

Chaos theory demonstrates that deterministic systems can exhibit unpredictable, sensitive-to-initial-conditions behavior. The "edge of chaos" hypothesis—that computational capacity is maximized at the boundary between ordered and chaotic dynamics—has been influential in both neuroscience and artificial life research.
