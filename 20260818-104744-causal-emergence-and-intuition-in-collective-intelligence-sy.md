## Key Findings

### 1. Collective Intelligence as a Multi-Level Phenomenon

The literature review by Woolley et al. (arXiv:1204.3401) identifies three levels of abstraction in collective intelligence research: individual cognition, group interaction dynamics, and emergent system-level properties. This aligns with the causal emergence framework: macro-level collective behavior can exhibit higher effective information than micro-level agent actions, even when the micro-level is fully determined by local rules.

**Implication for intuition**: Intuitive judgments in collective settings may operate at the macro-level, where patterns of coordination (not individual decisions) carry the causal information. This supports the hypothesis that intuition is not merely individual but can be a property of the collective's emergent structure.

### 2. Causal Explanation in Multi-Agent Systems (CEMA)

The CEMA framework (arXiv:2302.10809) provides a method for generating causal natural language explanations of agent decisions in dynamic sequential multi-agent systems without assuming a fixed causal structure. This is directly relevant to causal emergence: if macro-level causal structure is not fixed but emerges from agent interactions, then explanation methods must adapt dynamically.

**Connection to intuition**: Intuitive reasoning often bypasses explicit causal models. CEMA's approach of learning causal structure from observed dynamics mirrors how intuitive agents may internalize emergent causal patterns without explicit representation.

### 3. Communication as a Causal Coupling Mechanism

The survey of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning with communication (arXiv:2203.08975) demonstrates that communication broadens agents' views of the environment and supports collaboration. From a causal emergence perspective, communication channels create additional causal links between agents, potentially increasing the system's effective information at the collective level.

**Key insight**: When agents communicate, the macro-level state space expands beyond the product of individual states—new collective states emerge that have no micro-level equivalent. This is a concrete mechanism for causal emergence in multi-agent systems.

### 4. Systems Theory Foundations

Classical systems theory (Wikipedia) emphasizes that systems are cohesive groups of interrelated, interdependent components. The distinction between the system and its environment, and the concept of feedback loops, provide the mathematical substrate for understanding how micro-level interactions produce macro-level causal structure.

The sociotechnical system literature highlights that human and technical components form integrated systems where causal influence flows bidirectionally. This is relevant to human-AI collective intelligence: intuition may arise at the interface between human and machine agents.

### 5. Synchronicity and Acausal Connection

Jung's concept of synchronicity—events that coincide in time and appear meaningfully related yet lack discoverable causal connection—offers a provocative lens. In complex multi-agent systems, apparent "synchronicities" may actually be instances of causal emergence where the causal chain operates at a level not visible to individual agents. What appears acausal to the micro-level observer is fully causal at the macro level.

## Synthesis: Intuition as Macro-Level Causal Access

Drawing together these threads, a coherent picture emerges:

1. **Causal emergence** posits that macro-level descriptions can have higher effective information than micro-level descriptions (following Hoel's framework).
2. **Collective intelligence** research shows that group-level properties are not reducible to individual capabilities.
3. **Communication** in multi-agent systems creates new collective state spaces with emergent causal structure.
4. **Intuition** may be the cognitive faculty that accesses macro-level causal patterns directly, without requiring micro-level reconstruction.

**Hypothesis**: In collective intelligence systems, intuitive judgments serve as heuristics that approximate macro-level causal structure. When individual agents trust their intuition in coordination tasks, they are implicitly accessing emergent patterns that would be computationally expensive to derive from micro-level observation. This explains why intuition sometimes outperforms explicit reasoning in complex collective settings: explicit reasoning operates at the micro-level where causal information is diluted, while intuition operates at the macro-level where causal structure is concentrated.
