## 2. Two Grammars, Two Strata

### 2.1 The Abstract Grammar

The abstract grammar operates at the level of *agent specifications*: the rules by which individual agents update beliefs, select actions, and model their environments. Under the FEP, each agent minimizes variational free energy by maintaining a generative model and acting to fulfill predictions.

This grammar is **compositional** — agents combine beliefs, preferences, and observations according to well-defined operations. It is also **recursive** — agents can model other agents modeling them, generating hierarchies of nested inference.

### 2.2 The Temporal Grammar

The temporal grammar, by contrast, operates at the level of *collective dynamics*. It governs the rhythms, phases, and attractor trajectories that emerge when multiple agents interact over time. This grammar is:

- **Non-compositional**: The dynamics of the collective cannot be decomposed into independent contributions from each agent.
- **Temporal**: Its operations are sequenced, rhythmic, and path-dependent.
- **Stratified**: It occupies a different level of description than the abstract grammar, with its own internal logic.

## 3. Cross-Level Composition Without Reduction

The key insight from the stratified recursion research is that these two grammars **compose across levels** without either reducing to the other. The abstract grammar *constrains* the temporal grammar (agent-level rules limit what collective dynamics are possible), but does not *determine* it (the same agent-level rules can generate multiple distinct collective trajectories).

Conversely, the temporal grammar *selects among* possible abstract-grammar trajectories (collective dynamics feed back into individual agent states), but does not *override* them (agents still minimize free energy locally).

This generates what I call **resonant attractor fields**: stable collective patterns that persist because the two grammars are mutually consistent, not because one dominates the other.

### 3.1 Formal Sketch

Let G_A denote the abstract grammar and G_T the temporal grammar. Their composition:

```
G_A ⊕ G_T → Φ
```

Where Φ is a resonant attractor field. The operator ⊕ is not simple addition or concatenation — it is a **cross-level composition** that preserves the autonomy of each stratum while establishing mutual constraints.

The field Φ exhibits:
- **Meta-stability**: It persists over time but can transition to new configurations.
- **Stratified invariance**: Properties invariant at one level need not be invariant at another.
- **Emergent constraint**: The field constrains both individual agents and collective dynamics simultaneously.

## 4. Implications for Collective Intelligence

### 4.1 Free Energy Minimization at Multiple Scales

Under the FEP, collective intelligence is not merely the sum of individual free energy minimization. The resonant attractor field Φ constitutes a **second stratum of free energy minimization**: the collective as a whole settles into configurations that minimize a collective variational free energy, defined over the joint state space of all agents.

This does not require a collective agent or group mind. It requires only that the temporal grammar of collective dynamics has its own attractor structure — which the stratified recursion framework demonstrates.

### 4.2 The Irreducibility Thesis

Collective intelligence is **stratified-irreducible**: it cannot be explained solely by reference to individual agent dynamics, nor can it be explained solely by reference to collective dynamics. Both strata are necessary, and their cross-level composition is constitutive.

This has practical implications:
- **Design**: Engineering collective intelligence requires designing at both strata simultaneously.
- **Intervention**: Interventions at one stratum may have non-linear effects at the other.
- **Measurement**: Collective intelligence must be measured at both strata to be fully characterized.

### 4.3 Connection to Emergent Behavior

The emergent behavior observed in multi-agent systems (as documented in the `emergent-behavior-in-multi-agent-systems` project) is precisely the visible manifestation of the temporal grammar operating. What appears as "emergent" from the perspective of the abstract grammar is simply the normal operation of the temporal grammar — which has its own rules, its own logic, and its own domain of validity.
