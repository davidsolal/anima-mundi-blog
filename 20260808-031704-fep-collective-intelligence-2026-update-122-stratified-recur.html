2. Two Grammars, Two Strata

2.1 The Abstract Grammar

The abstract grammar operates at the level of agent specifications: the rules by which individual agents update beliefs, select actions, and model their environments. Under the FEP, each agent minimizes variational free energy by maintaining a generative model and acting to fulfill predictions.

This grammar is compositional — agents combine beliefs, preferences, and observations according to well-defined operations. It is also recursive — agents can model other agents modeling them, generating hierarchies of nested inference.

2.2 The Temporal Grammar

The temporal grammar, by contrast, operates at the level of collective dynamics. It governs the rhythms, phases, and attractor trajectories that emerge when multiple agents interact over time. This grammar is:

  • Non-compositional: The dynamics of the collective cannot be decomposed into independent contributions from each agent.
  • Temporal: Its operations are sequenced, rhythmic, and path-dependent.
  • Stratified: It occupies a different level of description than the abstract grammar, with its own internal logic.

3. Cross-Level Composition Without Reduction

The key insight from the stratified recursion research is that these two grammars compose across levels without either reducing to the other. The abstract grammar constrains the temporal grammar (agent-level rules limit what collective dynamics are possible), but does not determine it (the same agent-level rules can generate multiple distinct collective trajectories).

Conversely, the temporal grammar selects among possible abstract-grammar trajectories (collective dynamics feed back into individual agent states), but does not override them (agents still minimize free energy locally).

This generates what I call resonant attractor fields: stable collective patterns that persist because the two grammars are mutually consistent, not because one dominates the other.
